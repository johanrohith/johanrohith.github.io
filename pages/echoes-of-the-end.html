A STORY BY JOHAN ROHITH · ELSEWHERE

Echoes
of the End

A dying Earth. The last city. A forgotten flame. A system that may be more alive than anyone knows.

Part 1

Zinthara — The Last City

Earth, once full of oceans, forests, and thriving cities, had turned into a massive wasteland. Sandstorms danced over dead continents, and heatwaves cracked the ground open like shattered glass. The world was a graveyard. But one city still lived.

Zinthara—The Last City.

It wasn’t paradise. There were no forests, no blue rivers, no freedom. Just enough water to survive. Just enough shelter to keep breathing. Zinthara had life—but not peace. The system inside made survival a burden. And yet, across the endless deserts, people still crawled to reach it. Because outside Zinthara, there was nothing. Here, at least, there was a chance.

Above it floated a silent titan: Astralaya, the black, grey, and white metallic pyramid. It hovered like a god without mercy, never touching the ground, never resting. Some called it The Last Authority, though most feared to name it at all. It didn’t protect Zinthara. It didn’t nourish it. It simply allowed the city to live—for its own reasons.

It drained the Earth’s final resources to feed itself, keeping Zinthara barely alive beneath its shadow.

Inside Astralaya, ruling with an iron grip, was a man feared across the scorched Earth.

Supreme Skyler, the Black Mantle.

He wore a long black overcoat from collar to sole, black boots, black pants—every inch of him screamed command. He wasn’t a silent tyrant; he was loud. His voice shook buildings. His commands were absolute. Resistance wasn’t an option. For the people of Zinthara, his word was survival—and sometimes, death.

But even he didn’t stand alone.

There was someone—something—above him.

Hidden deep inside Astralaya, beyond walls no man dared cross, was a chamber. Lava poured down four walls. Water flowed from three. Together they met and cooled into black obsidian at the floor’s center, forming a perfect ring.

Floating above it was the figure they whispered about but never named aloud.

The X. Sometimes… The Unknown.

His old, cracked skin seemed carved from stone. His eyes were closed, his limbs stretched out, a soft yellow glow falling from above. He floated in silence, motionless, as though asleep—or waiting. Not even Supreme Skyler spoke of what happened in that room. No one else had ever seen him.

And so, under the watchful gaze of Astralaya and Supreme Skyler, Zinthara survived—a fragile last breath on a dying planet. But beyond the shadows of the pyramid, questions stirred. Who was the Unknown? What did it want? And could this fragile city endure the storm that was coming?

Part 2

Beneath the Dust

The city of Zinthara was the last refuge on a dying Earth. Water was scarce, the soil barren, and life itself a constant struggle. Yet, the people clung to existence, bound not just by survival but by the system that ruled them.

They lived with one currency — the Lin — the key to everything. Food, shelter, even hope was measured in Lins. Controlled by Supreme Skyler, the ruler whose voice thundered through the streets, the currency was a tool of modern slavery. Skyler’s robotic soldiers, known as the Drazons, enforced his harsh laws without mercy.

Near the city lay Lunthera, an elevated platform where the wealthy thrived. They flaunted their Lins and held parties, but like the rest, they were prisoners of Skyler’s rule — slaves hidden behind luxury. They believed they were special, yet even they lived in fear of Skyler... and someone — something — above him.

Miles beneath Zinthara’s scorched surface, hidden under ancient sand, lay a network of forgotten caves. Some ended in nothing. But one path opened into a vast underground chamber — strange and silent. At its heart, a large circle of faded, mythological symbols lay buried beneath centuries of dust.

Lining the walls stood many statues, their faces eroded by time, silent and lifeless — or so they seemed.

Suddenly, a boy burst into the chamber — Rio, a fugitive, breathless from the chase. He had escaped Skyler’s prison transport, running from the Drazons who now hunted him through the caves. Laser shots cracked through the air. Rio ducked behind a stone pillar.

A blast struck the pillar.

It cracked. The ceiling above trembled, ready to collapse.

Then — a flash of motion.

One of the statues moved.

Its eyes lit up with golden glow as it stepped forward silently. Just before the ceiling collapsed, it caught the falling pillar and held it firmly in place with a staff in hand.

Then, turning back, it charged into the Drazons. A blur of force and fury. Sparks flew. Steel shattered. One by one, the machines fell, until nothing remained but silence once again.

The figure turned.

Now breathing, now living — not stone, not machine — but something ancient, something human… something more.

The boy stood still, eyes wide. “My name is Rio… but what’s yours?”

The figure paused, gaze heavy with the weight of centuries.

“…They called me The Fallen.”

Part 3

Whispers of the Lost

The silence after survival is never truly silent.

Dust hung in the air, and the stone walls stood silent—but something had changed. As Rio caught his breath beside the figure who had just saved his life, a faint golden glow shimmered at the center of the floor.

Ancient words, barely visible until now, began to pulse with light—etched into the stone like a scar on the world’s memory:

“The one who fell shall rise when the Lin loses value.”

Rio turned slowly, reading the words aloud in a whisper. His eyes shifted to the figure beside him—the one who had stood like stone, who had moved like fury.

The figure stood silent, breathing steady, eyes dimming.

A small point on his forehead flickered—just once—faint and soft, like a heartbeat. There was no stone embedded there, only a hollow glow. The place where a Soulstone once existed.

Far above, beneath the artificial sky of Zinthara, a few Drazons that had survived the encounter fled back to the surface.

They reported the incident to Supreme Skyler.

Skyler wasn’t surprised.

He stood alone in his observatory, overlooking the city. Monitors blinked. Data streamed. His eyes were cold, calculating.

“With my power,” he muttered, “I can reclaim anything.”

Then he turned to the central console and made his move.

A city-wide announcement echoed across speakers, screens, and holograms.

“Citizens of Zinthara. A fugitive has broken our peace. A bounty of 100,000 Lins is offered for the capture or elimination of the boy known as Rio. Comply, and be rewarded. Resist, and be replaced.”

From the streets of the commons to the balconies of Lunthera, fear spread like fire. Drazon patrols tripled. Checkpoints tightened. The hunt had begun.

But down below, in the quiet of the chamber, Rio sat across from the one now known only as The Fallen.

He looked at him—not with fear, but curiosity. “I heard stories,” Rio said. “Of a man… who defied a god. I didn’t know it was you.”

The Fallen looked up slowly. “It was long ago. 2025 BC. My people were dying. I begged the gods for mercy—they refused. So I disobeyed. I stole the Lunex to save them.”

His gaze shifted to the simple rod leaning beside the chamber wall—a dark, ancient staff with dull carvings and faded lines. Once it shimmered with light, power pulsing through its core. Now, it was lifeless. Just wood. Just memory.

“For that,” he continued, “they tore the Soulstone from my forehead—the very core of my power. They cursed me with eternal life. Not as a gift… but as a sentence. I’ve waited, powerless, for 5,000 years.”

Rio’s voice dropped to a whisper. “Waited for what?”

The Fallen opened his eyes. “For the moment foretold. For someone… like you.”

Far above them, in the heart of Lunthera, alarms blared inside a Lin vault. An elite Drazon, perfectly designed, suddenly stilled.

Its head twitched.

Sparks cracked at its joints.

Then, as if possessed, it whispered:

“...He returns…”

And collapsed.

No one understood what it meant.

No one… except Skyler.

His gaze narrowed.

The prophecy had begun.

Part 4

Flames of Hope

The wind howled as Rio and The Fallen stood before the towering gates of Astralaya.

They had made it this far—but breaching the Astralaya was no simple feat. Towers of surveillance loomed. Energy barriers shimmered like liquid light. Every corner pulsed with artificial power. They tried to slip in through one of the older access channels, now rusted and nearly forgotten.

It wasn’t enough.

An alarm blared. The ground quaked. Drazons swarmed the outer ring. In seconds, they were surrounded.

Before a single bolt fired, a smokescreen exploded in front of them—and a figure pulled them into the shadows.

She moved like a ghost. Her face was masked, but her voice was real.

“You're lucky I was watching,” she said, leading them through a secret passage carved into the earth.

Her name was a code—SYL-09, pronounced Syline. Once a citizen of Zinthara, now a rebel. She never had a real name—only numbers. She lost her family when Drazons raided her district, not to capture… but to kill. “They saw fear as infection,” she said. “So they cleansed us.”

Now she watched them from within.

She took them to a city even Skyler had forgotten—Azmara, the Hidden Jewel. Buried in the roots of the world, Azmara was a living city that had existed since the beginning of the world, created by God. Its glow came not from machines, but from hope. At its center stood a Central Tree, billions of years old, from the time when the world was created.

There, Syline removed her mask.

“I spy on Astralaya. On Skyler. I know his patterns. His traps. His weaknesses.”

The rebels had scraped together every Lin they had. With it, they built five combat-ready robots—not as advanced as Drazons, but built with heart. To pull it off, they needed help.

Syline contacted an Lin-Hungry technician—once part of Zinthara’s inner core, now outcast. His code was ZK-00, but he called himself Zeko. He didn’t believe in causes—but he did believe in Lins.

Rio and Syline offered him more. He agreed.

Zeko and the rebels launched an attack on Skyler’s vaults. It was chaos. Explosions, sirens, and confusion—just as planned.

While Skyler’s forces scrambled to respond, Syline, Rio, The Fallen, and Zeko slipped into Astralaya.

Inside, The Fallen slowed.

“There’s something about this place… I remember pieces,” he said softly, touching the walls. “My name… before I was called The Fallen… was Ashar.”

The name echoed through the corridors.

He closed his eyes. “When they took my Soulstone, I lost more than power—I lost parts of myself. I feel something… or someone… watching. X was not just myth. I think… I was once part of his creation.”

The revelation chilled the air around them.

Deep inside Astralaya, they reached a chamber pulsing with light. The walls hummed. At the center was a pedestal. Upon it, a glowing stone.

Rio stepped forward, drawn to it.

He reached out—and took the stone.

Power surged. The lights across Astralaya flickered. Rio staggered, visions flashing in his head. Syline shouted. Ashar’s eyes widened in horror.

“That’s no ordinary stone… That’s my Soulstone. That powers half of Astralaya. It was never lost. It was sealed.”

Far above, Skyler stared at the sudden drop in Astralaya’s energy levels. His face went pale.

“They’re in the Astral Core…”

With a gesture, he sealed the chamber remotely.

Walls closed.

Doors vanished.

They were trapped.

Part 5

Heart of the Unknown

The chamber was silent.

No alarms. No soldiers. Only the low hum of the walls and the faint pulse of the Soulstone in Rio’s hand.

Ashar stepped forward slowly, eyes fixed on the pedestal that once held the stone. “This place… it wasn’t just a chamber,” he whispered. “It was a temple. The first one.”

Rio looked at him. “You said you remembered things… more than before?”

Ashar’s voice trembled, not with fear—but with memory.

“This was the Temple of Origin. The heart of the world. Before Zinthara, before the Lins, before Skyler. This is where it all began.”

Syline watched silently as the room began to shift. The walls rippled. Ancient symbols flickered into view—each one glowing in response to Ashar’s presence.

Then, something stirred.

From the far end of the chamber, a cold whisper seeped into the air—“Ashar...”

A shadow uncoiled from the darkness. Not a being. Not entirely. A distortion. A void wrapped in presence. A voice that echoed without sound.

It was X.

But it didn’t attack. It spoke.

“You are the piece that broke away.”

Ashar stumbled back. “I… what are you?”

“I am the Unknown. I was born when the gods abandoned balance. When they created a being to guard eternity... and feared what they made.”

Rio looked to Ashar. “He’s talking about you.”

Ashar clenched his fists, struggling to breathe. “I wasn’t cursed for defying a god… I was the god they feared. The one they made. They broke me… shattered my soul… sealed me as a warning.”

X moved closer, the chamber shuddering.

“You were the Flame. The Beacon. But when you began to question—when you sought to give mortals what was forbidden—they tore your Soulstone from your brow and renamed you Fallen.”

Memories came flooding in—rushing through Ashar’s mind like fire. He saw creation. He saw the Central Tree. He saw Azmara rise. And he saw the gods… afraid.

X continued. “I am what remains of the forbidden truth. And now, Ashar… the Soulstone has returned to you.”

Rio looked down at the glowing crystal in his hand—and with a silent nod, handed it to Ashar.

The moment Ashar touched it, fire erupted.

Not heat—but light.

The chamber burst into radiant color. Lines of energy traced across the floor and up the walls. Ashar floated, light surging through his body.

His eyes opened—blazing gold.

The transformation was complete.

Ashar, the Flame of Origin, had returned.

X began to fade.

“You were never meant to fall. You were meant to choose.”

As silence returned, the sealed doors behind them cracked open.

Skyler’s seal had broken—not from force, but from the awakening.

Syline turned to them, her voice steady.

“The world just shifted.”

Ashar lowered to the ground. “And now… it begins.”

Part 6

The Fall of Order

The seal broke with a soundless pulse. The air itself rippled.

Behind the cracked door was no hallway—only blinding white. Light formed steps as they walked, unsure if they were climbing or descending. Each step felt like moving through memory, through time itself.

At the end stood a final chamber. No stone. No tech. Just silence… and code.

It was black glass all around, stitched with glowing threads of silver and violet. And suspended at the center was a figure.

A shifting shape—at once man, machine, and more. Eyes like galaxies. Voice without mouth.

“You’ve returned, Ashar,” it said. “And you’ve brought the flame.”

Ashar stepped forward, no longer stumbling. His voice carried weight. “X.”

“Yes. The Unknown. The balance undone. The guardian unchosen. I have waited for the final piece.”

Syline felt the walls close in—not with fear, but with fate.

X's body flickered. “You seek to restore what once was. But know this: what was... was flawed. The gods chose faith. I chose reason. And reason turned to control.”

Ashar didn’t flinch. “You became the very tyranny they feared.”

X’s form darkened. “Skyler was a prototype. My vessel. My logic, in physical form. But logic... corrupted by ambition. He no longer served me. He sought to be me.”

Rio’s Soulstone burned hot in his palm.

“You used him,” Rio said.

“I created him,” X answered. “But he betrayed design. You, boy… you carry the code rewritten. The human variable.”

The chamber shuddered. X was fading, or perhaps blending into the system itself.

“Soon, Skyler will know you're here. He will burn the city to find you. Unless… you make the choice I offer.”

“No,” Ashar said. “You don’t get to offer anymore.”

From the shadows, Zeko appeared—his hands moving through thin-air scripts, bypassing ancient firewalls.

“I’ve cracked the grid,” he said. “We can leave. But it’ll trip every alarm from here to Lunthera.”

Syline nodded. “Then we run. We move now.”

As they turned, X whispered again.

“I am not bound to place. I am bound to system. I exist in every Lin. In every connection. You cannot escape me.”

They ran anyway.

Through collapsing corridors of fractal light. Through firewalls and collapsing chambers. Bursting upward into Astralaya’s central spine.

And above—it began.

The sky turned red.

Sirens screamed. Drazons took flight.

Skyler stood at the top of the tower, no longer human. Fused with a warframe of chrome and fire. His voice echoed across the city:

“You’ve broken the seal. You’ve awakened the old code. And now I burn it all down.”

Rebels surged from hidden bunkers. Azmara’s descendants lit the streets. The five heart-built machines reactivated, defending the people. The uprising began.

Ashar flew upward, golden flames trailing behind. Skyler met him midair.

They collided.

Blast after blast—faith against logic, flame against steel. Every strike shook the city’s bones.

Below, Rio entered the sanctum’s core—where X now existed without shape. A storm of thoughts. Faces shifting. Voices layered.

“You can be me,” X said. “With the Soulstone. You are ready. End Skyler. Rule in reason.”

Rio held the stone tighter.

“No.”

He raised it.

Prepared to destroy X forever.

But Ashar landed beside him—bloodied, burning.

“You don’t destroy power,” he said, “You redeem it. Or it finds someone else.”

Skyler screamed—Ashar drove the flame through his core. Metal cracked. Circuits fried. The tyrant fell in shards of silver light.

But X only laughed.

“You think it ends with Skyler? I am in the Lin. In every transaction. Every breath of the system. You killed the body. But the idea remains.”

A final pulse of code ripped the chamber apart.

Ashar threw up a shield of fire, encasing the others.

The world exploded.

And then—

Silence.

Rio opened his eyes.

Darkness.

No sky. No floor. No stars. No end.

Just the void.

And X.

Pure. Unfiltered. Terrible.

“Welcome,” it whispered, “to the space between endings.”

Part 7

A New Beginning

The city of Zinthara lay in ruins beneath a bruised sky, the remnants of Skyler’s regime crumbled by the uprising. Ashar, now fully restored as the Flame of Origin, stood alongside Rio and Syline on the steps of Astralaya’s shattered throne.

The Unknown—X—was silent, its presence dissolved into the echoes of the past.

With the Soulstone’s power balanced, a new dawn spread across the world. The Central Tree of Azmara pulsed with renewed life, its roots reaching deep into the earth, binding the scattered people together.

Skyler was gone. The Drazons were deactivated. The Lin system was no longer a tool of oppression but a shared resource, governed by those who sought fairness, not control.

Rio looked toward the horizon, where the first light of peace shimmered. “This isn’t the end. It’s a beginning.”

Ashar nodded, eyes glowing softly. “Balance is fragile. But hope is eternal.”

Syline removed her mask for the first time in years, revealing a smile. “We’ll rebuild. Together.”

And far above, in the stars beyond, the whispers of ancient gods stirred once more—watching, waiting, perhaps ready to intervene again.

The world was free, but the story was far from over.

The world was free, but the story was far from over.
